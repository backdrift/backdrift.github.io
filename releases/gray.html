Gray is my first album/EP. It's a collection of random songs I had made within a span of 5-6 months. I consider it to be an EP, but it is listed
as an album on all platforms due to the number of songs. It was my first attempt at making electronic/ambient music. I had previously made more
rock-oriented music, such as shoegaze and math rock. I found it easier to make ambient music, but also more fulfilling.

"7"
... was made using a Behringer Poly D Analog Synthesizer, as a matter of fact, a lot of songs on this album were. It's an ambient piece
that has a build up with lots of reverb and space. The name does not have much meaning besides trying to copy Aphex Twin's RDJ album opener.

"the day before"
This song, like a lot of my songwriting, was my attempt at making my version of [INSERT SONG]. In this case, Boards of Canada's "In the Annexe". Like
every song on this album, I used GarageBand as my DAW of choice. The reason I mention my DAW is because I wrote the song, then closed my laptop. I didn't
come back to it until the next day. However, I like to be precise about what days I wrote what songs. So, to remember when I wrote the song, I called
the project, "the day before". And it stuck.

"Enol"
... is a short jam song, inspired by Radiohead's Idioteque, but with worse sounding drums. I just added lots of overdrive onto digital presets,
rather than using an analog drum machine or modular synthesizer. The name was just Brian Eno's last name with an l added at the end, not much else to it.

"Mirrors"
This song was inspired by Brian Eno's The Big Ship. I am always chasing making my version of my favorite songs, although I never reach that goal.

"Monoamine"
This song was inspired by Aphex Twin's #3/Rhubarb. The song title has no meaning other than I liked the way it sounded.
It is an ambient work that has lots of reverb and a pad that swells in repitition.

"Poly"
... was made using the Behringer Poly D Analog Synthesizer. Every sound besides the kick was created on this synthesizer. Of course I arranged the
structure and effects using GarageBand. My favorite part of the song is the chord progression and how it interacts with the main melody.

"Memories"
... is one of my favorite songs I've made. It has a nostalgic and melancholic feel to it. It aged better than some of my other songs since at first I didn't appreciate
it as much. Over the years I grew to like it much more. The songs main bass line was inspired by Radiohead's All I Need.
It has one of my favorite endings to my songs.

"Waiting"
This song sounds like a clock. It sounds like time passing by.
